## How to Use This Document

This document is the single source of truth for the AI Coach's knowledge base. It contains all articles the AI draws from when building personalized fitness plans and answering user questions.

### Structure
- **Part A — Website Articles (18):** Curated articles visible to site visitors, organized by pyramid section
- **Part B — Background Articles (9):** Internal-only articles that give the AI deeper knowledge to build better programs — never surfaced to visitors

### How to Update
1. Find a new high-quality article relevant to a section
2. Add it to the correct section in Part A or Part B
3. Fill in all fields: Title, Source, URL, Summary, Key Facts, and Date Added
4. Increment the version number at the top of this document
5. Note the reason for the update in the Version History at the bottom

### Update Triggers
- New major research published (e.g. updated US dietary guidelines)
- Existing article URL goes dead (replace with equivalent)
- A section needs stronger coverage of a specific angle
- Quarterly review — check for more current versions of existing articles

### B9 — Cortisol & Overtraining

**Article 27**
- **Title:** Overtraining: 9 Signs of Overtraining to Look Out For
- **Source:** ACE Fitness (American Council on Exercise)
- **Authority:** Leading US Exercise Science Organization
- **URL:** https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/6466/overtraining-9-signs-of-overtraining-to-look-out-for/
- **Purpose:** Helps the AI recognize and respond to users showing signs of overtraining or excessive cortisol
- **Key Facts for AI:**
  - Overtraining syndrome (OTS): chronic fatigue from imbalance between training and recovery
  - 9 signs of overtraining:
    1. Decreased performance despite increased effort
    2. Increased perceived effort during workouts
    3. Excessive fatigue
    4. Agitation, moodiness, irritability
    5. Insomnia or disrupted sleep
    6. Loss of motivation
    7. Increased injuries
    8. Increased illness (suppressed immune system)
    9. Appetite suppression or weight loss
  - Cortisol stays elevated with overtraining — interferes with muscle growth and fat loss
  - For office workers: workplace stress compounds training stress — reduce intensity, not eliminate
  - Recovery from OTS: rest, reduce intensity, prioritize sleep and nutrition
  - Beginners especially prone — always start conservatively
  - If user describes multiple OTS signs: recommend de-loading (reduce volume 40–50%) before continuing
- **Date Added:** May 2026

## Article Quality Standards

When adding new articles, all entries must meet these standards:

| Standard | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Source authority** | Medical institution, government body, peer-reviewed journal, or recognized expert publication |
| **US relevance** | Article must be US-based or directly applicable to US office workers |
| **Recency** | Prefer articles from 2023 onwards; flag any article older than 5 years for review |
| **Accessibility** | Article must be publicly accessible — no paywalls |
| **Specificity** | Prefer articles that address office workers, desk workers, or busy professionals specifically |
| **No conflict of interest** | Avoid articles from brands with direct commercial interest in the recommendation (e.g. a supplement company recommending supplements) |
